### Research

I work on evaluation methodology, taking inspiration from language and cognitive sciences. My work follows three connected questions:

- **What should we measure?** What it means to evaluate "intelligence" or a specific capability in a model, and whether better-defined constructs, like generality, make for sounder evaluation.
- **How reliable are our measurements?** How results shift when the evaluation setup changes, and whether psychometric methods hold up across benchmark data and languages.
- **How should evaluations be surfaced?** How evaluation reporting can be made transparent and useful for research and governance.

Some of the work behind those questions:


- **[Evaluating Adjective-Noun Compositionality in LLMs: Functional vs Representational Perspectives](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09994)** — the same capability question gives you different answers depending on whether you test behaviour or inspect representations (\*SEM, ACL 2026).
- **Does Item Response Theory Generalize Across Languages? Evidence from Cross-Lingual LLM Benchmarks** — whether psychometric methods for efficient evaluation still hold when the benchmark data changes language (forthcoming in EMNLP Findings, 2026).
- **[On the Measure of a Model: From Intelligence to Generality](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.11773)** — "intelligence" is too elusive a construct to anchor evaluation; generality, i.e. how consistently a model performs across a range of tasks, gives us something we can actually define and measure (EurIPS Workshop on the Science of Benchmarking and Evaluating AI 2025).
- **[EvalCards: A Framework for Standardized Evaluation Reporting](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.21695)** — a reporting format that makes evaluation results auditable and interpretable for research, industry, and policy audiences, rather than merely comparable between models (EurIPS Workshop on Evaluating AI 2025; Nordic AI Meet 2026).

At my internship, I'm also looking into safety and security evaluation for agentic systems: building an evaluation harness for AI agents, and working on privacy evaluations, prompt injection, and red teaming under real regulatory requirements.
